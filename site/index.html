The problem with notes

Rereading feels like studying. Testing yourself is studying.

Learning research is unusually clear about what works. Static notes support almost none of it.

High utility

Practice testing and spaced practice were the two techniques rated most useful, out of ten common study techniques reviewed. Highlighting and rereading rated low.

Dunlosky et al., 2013 · Psychological Science in the Public Interest
g ≈ 0.85

Simulation-based learning showed large effects across 145 studies in higher education, and helped most when the simulation was guided.

Chernikova et al., 2020 · Review of Educational Research
g ≈ 0.48

Studying worked examples improved mathematics performance, especially when learners explain each step to themselves.

Barbieri et al., 2023 · Educational Psychology Review

So the built-in card types are those techniques, ready to drop into any lesson:

Quiz → retrieval practice Practice Set → varied, repeated practice Guided Explore → guided simulation Worked Example → self-explanation

How it works

Twelve building blocks. Infinite lessons.

A card is a small stack of slots. Slots in the same card share variables, so a slider drives a formula, the formula drives a chart, and the text quotes the result. Tap the blocks to build a card.

Variables stay inside their card, so one card can never break another. Cycles and missing values are caught when you import.

New notebook from AI

Any chat AI drafts it. You stay the author.

  1. Copy the promptNoteBuilder writes it for you: the format, every slot with an example, the formula rules.
  2. Paste the replyCode fences and chatter around the JSON are fine.
  3. See what to fixEach problem says where it is, what is wrong and how to fix it, with a fix request to paste back.
  4. Review the draftsEvery AI-made card arrives as a draft. Share reminds you while drafts remain.
Works with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or any chat AI. No AI runs inside the app.

Practice problems

Fresh numbers every attempt.

You write the problem once with ranges and a formula. Every attempt draws new numbers, so students practise the idea, not the answer.

Each student gets their own sequence, so one shared answer list doesn't help anyone.

Peek at an answer and those numbers earn no credit. Done means done for real.

Sharing

One file. Opens anywhere. Remembers everything.

  • No install, no account, no internet. Email it, drop it in your course page, put it on a USB stick. It opens in any browser on any phone or laptop.
  • Progress stays with the student. Answers, ticks and practice streaks are saved in their browser and come back when they reopen the file.
  • My results for the teacher. Students copy or download a summary of their progress and graded answers, and send it to you.
  • Nothing is sent anywhere. The file itself makes no network requests, not even for fonts.

Saves you can trust

Every save is one atomic database transaction. Nothing is written until loading has finished, and saves flush when you switch away or close the window.

Version history

Mac app

Download the DMG from Releases (Apple silicon) and drag NoteBuilder to Applications. It isn't notarized yet, so the first launch takes one extra step: on macOS 15 or later, click Done, then Open Anyway in System Settings → Privacy & Security; on older macOS, right-click the app and choose Open.
